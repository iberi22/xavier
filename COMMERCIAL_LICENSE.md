# Xavier Commercial License

## Overview

Xavier is dual-licensed under the **GNU Affero General Public License v3 (AGPL-3.0)** 
and a **Commercial License**.

- **AGPL-3.0**: Free for everyone. Full source code access. Requires source disclosure for network services.
- **Commercial License**: Required when you need proprietary integration, closed-source modifications, or private mesh networks without source disclosure.

## When You Need a Commercial License

You need a Commercial License if you want to:

1. **Integrate Xavier into a proprietary product** — where the combined work is distributed without source
2. **Run a private mesh node** — without making your modifications public
3. **Modify Xavier's enterprise-reserved features** — advanced-rrf, enterprise-embeddings, advanced-audit-logging, priority-support-queue
4. **Avoid AGPL copyleft requirements** — for closed-source commercial deployment
5. **Get priority support and SLA** — guaranteed response times and dedicated engineering

## When You DON'T Need a Commercial License

The AGPL is sufficient if:

1. You use Xavier **as-is** internally (no modifications)
2. You run Xavier as a network service and **release your modifications** under AGPL
3. You contribute changes back to the community
4. You're an individual developer or open-source project
5. You're a commercial entity under $5M annual revenue using unmodified Xavier internally

## License Transition

After 3 years from the release date of each version, the Commercial License automatically
reverts to AGPL-3.0. This ensures Xavier's codebase always remains available as open source
in the long term, while protecting the business model for current development.
